G0

How the questions were found

Two independent passes, and an assessment of the pre-existing gap document.

Part A · one-page method summary

Question
For a one-year project on swimming pools as power-to-heat flexibility in Schleswig-Holstein, what does the scholarly literature not know, as distinct from what the project merely has to collect? And is a pre-existing gap document pointing in the right direction?
Method, three stages
First, a topic-mode literature and policy review run against the job advertisement, producing a five-gap map with every finding labelled. Second, an assessment of the user's existing 286-line gap document against that map and against the verification method. Third, an independent second pass: a multi-perspective scan run in an isolated context that was given the advertisement and the method files but was explicitly forbidden from reading the first map, so that agreement between the two would mean something. Finally a three-way comparison, with the second pass's headline finding independently verified before acceptance.
Result
Five research gaps: fleet-scale aggregation and curtailment attribution; whether distribution-connected loads can reach the binding constraint at all; why an available regulatory instrument goes unused; the comfort and hygiene envelope as a formal flexibility bound; and willingness under real municipal governance. The second pass added three the first missed: the reference accounting's structural zero for baths, the evaporation loop, and the 2022/23 temperature episode as a natural experiment. The pre-existing document was assessed as the right territory at the wrong altitude, with roughly 100 unresolvable citation markers.
Status
Curtailment figures, the regulatory instrument's rules and the modelling stack VERIFIED from primary sources. Absence claims UNRESOLVED, by both passes' own admission, because neither ran a systematic search protocol.
Files
splash-pool-flexibility.md and its provenance; perspectives_splash.md; splash-gaps_ORIGINAL.md (the assessed document); splash-what-how-when.md; verification_log.md first and second passes; corrections C1 to C4.

Part B · the full trail, including the dead ends

B.1 Framing

  1. The inputs were the job advertisement (verbatim), a 286-line gap document written previously by the candidate, and an instruction to produce a research-gap map only, without interview framing.
  2. Two framing decisions were taken at the start and recorded in the plan. First, the target is a domain switch away from the default research domain, so the canon's rule on appending rather than overwriting a domain profile applies. Second, and central to everything that followed, a working definition: a work task is something the project must build or collect because it does not exist locally; a research gap is a question the field cannot answer, whose answer generalises beyond Schleswig-Holstein. Every candidate finding was tested against that distinction.
  3. The pre-existing document was copied to the programme folder unchanged before any analysis, so that the artifact under assessment could not drift.
  4. Scale decision, recorded: lead-owned direct search rather than delegation, because the question is narrow and specialist and a cold start would cost more than it returns. Minimum three query angles per key question.

B.2 First pass: what the field already knows

  1. Nine searches across six angles: pool thermal storage and demand response; municipal pool energy consumption in Germany; power-to-heat against curtailment; the German regulatory instrument; the Flensburg group's own modelling; and demand-response acceptance among institutional operators.
  2. The prior work on pools as a demand-response asset was found to be thin, Nordic, simulation-based and price-driven: one Finnish swimming hall, an energy-use study of Norwegian halls, a pool-as-cooling-storage study, and an aquatic-centre operating-data benchmark. This established the opening the project occupies, and corrected the pre-existing document's implicit framing that pools are unstudied.
  3. Four fetches were attempted on the closest paper and its repository record; both returned 403. Its citation was VERIFIED from two independent listings and its quantitative results marked UNRESOLVED, a status that persisted through every later pass.
  4. The state's grid-congestion report was fetched and produced the pass's load-bearing finding: on-land curtailment fell from about 3,350 GWh in 2019 to 825 GWh in 2025, and over 65% of it is ordered by the upstream transmission operator for congestion partly outside the state. This reframed the advertised geospatial package from a data problem into a question about where in the network topology a distribution-connected load can relieve a constraint, and became R2.
  5. The regulatory instrument was fetched from an independent assessment: eligibility segments including large heat pumps, additionality conditions, a trial from October 2024, and the finding that the instrument had essentially not been used despite available supply. This became R5's starting point. The reported phase-3 date could not be confirmed (the source deck would not parse) and was carried at [verify] for six passes until R2 closed it from the operators' own concept.

B.3 First pass: assessing the pre-existing document

  1. The document was read in full and assessed on four axes. Structure: its work-package decomposition tracks the advertisement accurately, and several individual observations are sharp. Altitude: most of its roughly 45 items describe what the project must do rather than what the field does not know, which is the central defect; most convert with reframing, and examples of the conversion were given. Citations: roughly 100 markers of the form [web:N] with no source list anywhere in 286 lines, so none resolves and none may enter a deliverable. Omissions: the regulatory instrument entirely absent, the curtailment premise not interrogated, and the prior pool literature missing.
  2. One factual contradiction with the advertisement was found and logged (C2): the document describes a half-time post; the advertisement is full-time.

B.4 The independence problem, and how it was handled

  1. The question was raised whether a multi-perspective scan should be run on the gaps. The method file's own skip conditions were applied: it says to skip the scan where a single verified source is what is needed, and the open items at that point were verification tasks, not framing tasks. A further objection was recorded: a scan run in the same context as the first map would inherit its framing, and the method warns that personas share one model's priors, so their agreement is not confirmation.
  2. When an independent pass was nonetheless requested, the isolation was made structural rather than a matter of self-restraint. A second pass was run in a fresh context that received only the advertisement verbatim and the two method files, and was explicitly forbidden from opening the first map, its provenance, its plan, the verification log or the pre-existing document. Web search was left unrestricted on the stated reasoning that reaching the same public sources independently is convergent evidence, while reading the first pass's conclusions is contamination.
  3. The second pass confirmed in its output that the isolation held and that it had seen none of the first pass's material.

B.5 Second pass: what it added

  1. It ran a five-lens research cast, a contradiction map and a self peer-review, and reported its own dominant lens as the skeptic, noting that a scan run to find gaps is structurally predisposed to a deficit narrative, and that its practitioner section was its weakest because it rested on trade sources with no operating data from a single German pool.
  2. Convergence with the first pass, reached independently: the same curtailment figures and their upstream cause; the same conclusion that the geospatial package measures the wrong adjacency; the same finding that the prior literature is single-asset and price-driven; and the same regulatory instrument, though ranked fourth rather than first.
  3. Three findings the first pass had missed. A reference demand-response accounting table scoring baths at zero flexible electricity for a methodological reason, which became R1. The coupled water, humidity and electricity loop, which became R4 and which the first pass had predicted as its own likely blind spot in the engineering direction. And the winter 2022/23 fleet-wide temperature reduction as an existing natural experiment, which became R3 and which remains unworked because it requires operator contact.
  4. It also surfaced a detail that later corrected the first pass's reading of the regulatory instrument: an exclusion tied to combined heat and power contracts. That correction was itself later narrowed when the statute was read directly in R5 (C12).

B.6 Verifying the second pass before accepting it

  1. Its headline finding was checked rather than taken on trust. The source PDF was located and text-extracted locally after two browser routes failed, and the table row was read directly. The finding was confirmed, and two corrections followed: the claim that baths were the only zero was wrong (laundries is also zero), and the citation year and an author's name were wrong. Logged as C3 and C4.
  2. The second pass had itself logged a correction of the same kind in its own record: it initially suspected a search summary of fabricating a claim, then retrieved the full paper and found the claim correct and specific, recording the lesson that an abstract-page miss is not disproof. This was taken as evidence that the pass was run honestly.

B.7 Three-way comparison and sequencing

  1. The comparison was drawn explicitly, with the epistemic caveat stated: convergence between two passes of the same underlying model means a finding is reachable by more than one route, not that it is true; the informative signal is the disagreement. Both passes independently failed to find any public description of the project itself, so all analysis rests on the advertisement.
  2. The gaps were then sequenced against the project calendar into a what, how and when document, which introduced the Tier 0 questions (is there an addressable empirical object; does an inventory exist) and identified the structural mismatch between six advertised work packages and four master's theses.
  3. When work moved from identifying gaps to answering them, the order was chosen by what could be answered without proprietary data: R1 first (needs only public tables), then R4 (published coefficients), then Q0.2 (public administrative records), then R2 (which Q0.2 unblocked), then R5 (primary legal texts). R3 remains unworked because it requires contacting operators.

B.8 How the conclusions were derived

The gap set is the product of three filters applied in order. The work-task versus research-gap distinction (step 2) removed most of the pre-existing document's items and most of the advertisement's packages. Primary-source verification (steps 8, 9, 19) removed or re-labelled findings that could not be traced, including one of the second pass's own headline claims. And structural independence (step 13) rather than self-restraint was used to test whether the first pass's framing was an artifact, which it partly was: three of the five worked questions originate in the second pass, and one of them (R4) was the blind spot the first pass had predicted in itself but not filled.

B.9 Final conclusion

The project as advertised is largely a collection exercise; its research content lies underneath it, in five questions. Two passes run independently agree on the two structural ones (the constraint geography, and fleet scale versus single asset) and disagree usefully on the rest, with the second pass supplying the methodological gap that became the strongest single result of the whole exercise. The pre-existing gap document is the right territory at the wrong altitude, with citations that cannot be used; its structure and several of its observations survive reframing. The largest identified gap, what public-service quality is spent per unit of flexibility and which user group bears it, remains unworked and is scoped as beyond a one-year project.

Status of the conclusion. VERIFIED as to the primary-source findings that anchor each gap. UNRESOLVED as to every absence claim, by both passes' own admission: the correct formulation is "not found", never "does not exist". The gap set is a set of options, not a selection; choosing among them is not this method's function.

B.10 What would change it

A documented systematic search protocol, which would convert the absence claims from UNRESOLVED to either established or refuted; a public description of the project, which neither pass could find; and reading the closest prior paper in full, which has been blocked at every attempt and which bears on at least two of the five gaps.

Q0.1

Addressable curtailment

Recorded as blocked for five passes. It was not.

Part A · one-page method summary

Question
What share of Schleswig-Holstein's curtailed renewable energy can a distribution-connected swimming pool actually reach? Recorded through five earlier passes as blocked on non-public network-operator data.
Sources
MEKUN, Stromnetz-Engpassmanagement in Schleswig-Holstein, 15 April 2026 (full report, not just the web page); Schleswig-Holstein Netz AG, Netzsituation und Besonderheiten (2017) and Netzausbauplan 2022 and 2024; § 13k Abs. 8 EnWG; the transmission operators' implementation concept v2.0 on the distribution-operator route; Bundesnetzagentur congestion-management tables Q4 2023; SMARD 2025 figures; OpenStreetMap substations for operator territories.
Method
Primary-source causation analysis rather than modelling. The causal statements of the network operator, the state ministry and the regulator were read verbatim and cross-checked against each other and against federal data. A composite arithmetic was then computed and labelled indicative. The spatial component was a county-level join of the 166-unit pool list to the operator's twelve medium-voltage congestion regions.
Result
Of 802 GWh curtailed on land in 2025, at least 521 GWh is ordered by the transmission operator; of the remainder, the network operator states that over 90% of its own cases are caused by extra-high-voltage unavailability rather than its own 110 kV network. That leaves on the order of 28 GWh, about 3%, genuinely distribution-caused. The distribution operators told the transmission operators at the end of 2025 that they see no case for their own §13k Abs. 8 instrument, which independently confirms the same conclusion.
The question inverts
Where a constraint is caused is not where a load must sit to relieve it. §13k exists precisely so that distribution-connected loads can absorb transmission-caused curtailment. So essentially all of the addressable volume is transmission-caused, reachable through §13k inside a relief region, and the unaddressable part is the small distribution residual.
Status
Every source statement VERIFIED by local extraction. The composite arithmetic is indicative, combining a 2026 volume share with a 2017 case share. Feeder-level assignment remains operator-held.
Files
q01_dso_congestion_join.py; q01_fag_pools_dso_congestion.csv; verification_log.md eighth pass; corrections C16, C17, C18.

Part B · the full trail, including the dead ends

B.1 Revisiting the blockage

  1. Q0.1 had been recorded in the sequencing document as a Tier 0 question, restated in R2 as still standing, and described in conversation as blocked on non-public SH Netz data. Before accepting that a second time, the claim was tested: what exactly would the missing data be, and is there genuinely nothing public that bears on it.
  2. Three source classes were identified as unpulled. The state report had only ever been read as a web page, not as the full PDF, and the "over 65%" figure in it is a causation attribution. Distribution operators have published redispatch measures since October 2021 under Redispatch 2.0. And the network operator's own 110 kV development plan, published under § 14d EnWG, must discuss where its congestion is.
  3. The assessment was recorded as too strong and the question reopened. Logged afterwards as C16.

B.2 The state report

  1. The full report was downloaded and extracted. It is four pages of text plus an annex and it carries considerably more than the web page. Beyond the 802 GWh and the 65% attribution it states that the overwhelming share of curtailment is triggered by transmission congestion, that on-land renewable generation reached an all-time high of 22.3 TWh in 2025 despite a poor wind year, that 3.6% of on-land renewable generation was curtailed against over 17% in 2015, that state-level cost attribution has been impossible since Redispatch 2.0 in 2021, and that relief from the large direct-current links is expected only after 2028.
  2. Two passages were noted for later use. The report calls for greater use of renewable output at lower network levels "z.B. durch zuschaltbare Lasten", and names flexible connection agreements for connecting to already-full networks. That is the state ministry asking for exactly the asset class the project studies.

B.3 The network operator's own documents

  1. A first download of the Netzausbauplan 2024 returned an HTML error page rather than a PDF. The request was retried with browser-like headers (user agent, accept, accept-language, referer), which returned the real file, 55 MB. The 2022 plan and the 2017 network-situation paper were retrieved the same way.
  2. All three carry the same causal statement. The 2017 paper quantifies it: the cause of these curtailments lies "in über 90 Prozent der Fälle nicht im 110-kV-Netz von SH Netz, sondern in der noch nicht ausreichend vorhandenen Verfügbarkeit des Höchstspannungsnetzes". The 2022 and 2024 plans repeat the statement without the percentage, so the direction is current and only the figure is dated.
  3. The same 2017 paper records that over 90% of the state's renewable capacity is connected to SH Netz networks, about 34,000 installations and over 8 GW, which establishes that this operator's statement is about the great majority of the state's curtailment rather than a corner of it.
  4. Per-county measure pages on the operator's site were attempted for seven counties. All returned 404 with a 158 kB error page. The network-information page returned 403. These were abandoned; the plan PDFs carry the same material.

B.4 The federal cross-check

  1. The Bundesnetzagentur congestion page was fetched and found to carry nothing later than 2023, directing users to SMARD for current data. The Q4 2023 tables were downloaded as a spreadsheet and read with openpyxl.
  2. Tabelle 4 gives the split that matters: redispatch with renewables, 1,657 GWh of measures at transmission level against 1,154.8 GWh at distribution level, while causation splits 2,193.1 GWh in the transmission network against 618.7 GWh in the distribution network. Tabelle 9 shows Schleswig-Holstein at 950.1 GWh of instructed reductions in that quarter, second only to Lower Saxony.
  3. The SMARD 2025 article was fetched for the current national picture: 30.3 TWh of measures at EUR 3.07 bn, 9.38 TWh of renewable curtailment, 3.5% of renewable generation curtailed, and a shift of causation toward distribution networks, about 35% in 2025 against 26% in 2024.
  4. This established an important contrast rather than a confirmation. Nationally, congestion causation is moving into the distribution networks. Schleswig-Holstein is the opposite case, the state where transmission causation dominates most heavily. The national trend does not transfer.

B.5 The decisive independent check

  1. § 13k Abs. 8 EnWG was read in the statute text already on disk. It permits distribution operators with 110 kV networks and more than 100,000 customers to run their own instrument from 1 April 2025, conditional on curtailment caused in their own network reaching a threshold over the previous two calendar years.
  2. The transmission operators' implementation concept was searched for their treatment of it and yielded a recorded outcome: the coordination with the distribution operators at the end of 2025 found that the distribution operators "absehbar keinen eigenständigen Anwendungsfall" for their own relief regions under § 13k Abs. 8.
  3. This was taken as the strongest single piece of evidence in the pass, because it is not a statement about causation at all but a revealed decision by the parties who would profit. Given a statutory route to monetise their own congestion, the distribution operators declined it. That agrees with the roughly 3% residual computed from the other sources.

B.6 The inversion

  1. At this point the original framing was re-examined. The first gap map had treated transmission causation as a threat, writing that a pool near a wind farm "may have no claim whatever" on that energy.
  2. The two questions were separated explicitly. Where a constraint is caused is a question about the network. Where a load must sit to relieve it is a question about power flow, and the answer is that a load consuming north of a bottleneck relieves that bottleneck whatever voltage level it hangs off. The instrument is built on exactly this: relief regions exist so that additional switchable loads, mostly distribution-connected, can absorb transmission-caused curtailment, and the concept's stated reason for including the Hamburg belt is that loads there reduce redispatch caused by bottlenecks south of Hamburg.
  3. The federal numbers corroborate the mechanism at scale: distribution-connected assets already carry 41% of renewable redispatch measures against 22% distribution-caused congestion.
  4. The conclusion therefore inverted. Transmission causation is the addressable market, not the obstacle, and R2's finding sharpens: relief-region membership is not a proxy for the right variable, it is the right variable. Logged as C17.

B.7 The spatial component, built twice

  1. The Netzausbauplan 2024 was searched for congestion structure and yielded Tabelle 6: twelve medium-voltage congestion regions for the 2045 scenario, each with the share of its line elements projected to be overloaded, and each named by county and Netzcenter.
  2. A first join geocoded the fifteen Netzcenter towns and measured each pool's distance to the nearest one, reporting 22 of 76 indoor units within 10 km and 40 within 15 km.
  3. That approach was then rejected on inspection. The congestion regions are county-shaped, so distance to the centre town adds nothing beyond county membership while implying a spatial precision the source does not carry. The script was rewritten to join by county and to report the projected overload share per county. The distance figures were discarded. Logged as C18.
  4. The rewritten join produced the finding that matters: the counties with the worst projected 2045 distribution congestion are not those with today's transmission-caused curtailment. Ostholstein tops the list at 72% with seven indoor pools, and it, Herzogtum Lauenburg (55%) and Plön (29%) are outside the § 13k relief regions entirely.
  5. Flensburg, Kiel and Lübeck do not appear in the operator's plan at all, because they are served by other distribution operators. The OpenStreetMap substation pull corroborated this through operator tags: SH Netz 106 substations at 110 kV and above, TraveNetz 18 in the Lübeck area, TenneT 12.

B.8 A robustness fix

  1. On a final verification run the script failed when invoked from outside its own folder, because every script in the set used paths relative to the working directory. All five were patched to resolve paths against their own location and re-run from the parent directory. This is not a finding but it matters for reproducibility, since the documents tell a reader to run them.

B.9 How the conclusion was derived

Three independent parties are on record about the same quantity: the network operator that performs the curtailment, the ministry that reports it, and the distribution operators collectively, who declined a statutory instrument that would only pay if the quantity were material. They agree, and they agree with the federal causation split for the country as a whole once the state's atypical position is accounted for. The composite arithmetic is then simple and its weakness is disclosed: it mixes a volume share from 2026 with a case share from 2017, and both are lower bounds on transmission causation, so the distribution residual is an upper bound rather than a point estimate. The inversion was not a new source but a re-reading of what the existing ones imply, and it was checked against the instrument's own stated design rationale rather than asserted.

B.10 Final conclusion

Essentially all of Schleswig-Holstein's curtailment is transmission-caused: at least 65% ordered by the transmission operator, and over 90% of the network operator's own remaining cases caused by extra-high-voltage unavailability rather than its own network, leaving on the order of 3% genuinely distribution-caused. That residual has no instrument, because the distribution operators have formally declined to create one. This is favourable rather than fatal for the project, because the transmission-caused portion is precisely what § 13k makes available to distribution-connected switchable loads inside a relief region. The addressable quantity is therefore the large majority of 802 GWh rather than a fraction of it, and the variable that decides access is regulatory designation, not geography. A second and separate value proposition exists on the 2045 horizon in the twelve projected medium-voltage congestion regions, strongest in three counties that cannot participate in § 13k today, and it runs through flexible connection agreements rather than through the tender.

Status of the conclusion. VERIFIED as to every underlying statement and as to the inversion, which follows from the instrument's own design. ESTIMATE as to the roughly 3% residual, labelled indicative with its two vintages disclosed. UNRESOLVED as to feeder-level assignment for any individual facility.

B.11 What would change it

A current quantification from SH Netz of curtailment caused in its own network, replacing the 2017 figure; the load-versus-generation trigger split for the twelve congestion regions, which decides whether an added load in each region helps or worsens the projection; a change of position by the distribution operators on § 13k Abs. 8, which would create an instrument for the residual; and feeder-level data, which alone can move the question from county scale to facility scale.

Q0.2

The pool inventory

Three failed parses, then a reconciliation to 99.93%.

Part A · one-page method summary

Question
The project's data-acquisition package assumes the inventory must be built from nothing. Does a usable inventory exist, at what quality, and what is genuinely missing?
Sources
Landtag Drucksache 20/4417 of 29 April 2026, the state government's answer on swimming facilities, with Anlage 1 (Sportstättenerhebung 2019, Statistikamt Nord) and Anlage 2 (the 2025 FAG swimming-lesson funding list); OpenStreetMap via Overpass; Bäderleben (the national pool database); UBA TEXTE 139/2024 for per-pool heat.
Method
The Drucksache was downloaded and extracted in two modes. Anlage 2 was parsed into a table and reconciled against its own printed totals as a correctness test. Anlage 1's pool rows were read for water surface, basin counts and ownership. An Overpass query pulled every pool-related element inside the state boundary, which was cleaned, clustered into facilities and scored for attribute coverage. The 2019 counts were corrected for the survey's stated 71.3% response rate and compared with the 2025 record as a consistency check before any carry-forward was proposed.
Result
An address-level administrative list of every publicly funded pool exists and is current to 2025: 166 funded units, about 153 facilities, 107 administrative units, reconciling to the printed totals at 99.93%. The 2019 survey supplies water surface (76,488 m²), basin counts and ownership at 71% response. No technical inventory exists anywhere. The state says in writing that its own data are inadequate and announces an expanded Statistikamt survey at the end of 2026. Only nine municipalities describe their heat supply, in free text.
Status
All source figures VERIFIED by local extraction. The 2019-to-2025 reconciliation and the water-surface carry-forward are labelled estimates resting on a stated non-response assumption. The indoor share of water surface is UNRESOLVED.
Limits
The FAG list covers only pools receiving swimming-lesson funding; OSM includes hotel and club pools and carries no technical attributes; the Bäderleben extract was not obtained by script.
Files
q02_fag_pools.py, q02_osm_pools.py, q02_fag2025_sh_pools.csv (166 rows), q02_osm_sh_facilities.csv (239 rows); verification_log.md fifth pass; corrections C7, C8.

Part B · the full trail, including the dead ends

B.1 Origin

  1. Q0.2 was one of the two Tier 0 questions in the sequencing document: everything downstream consumes the inventory, and if it does not exist the geospatial and modelling packages have no population. It was selected after R1 and R4 because it needs no proprietary data and it unblocks R2.

B.2 First pulls

  1. Four pulls in parallel: an Overpass query for pool-related tags inside the DE-SH boundary; a search for state-level pool counts; a search for the Bäderleben database; and a search for state pool funding programmes.
  2. Overpass returned 1,180 elements. The tag distribution showed the cleaning problem immediately: 285 elements tagged access=private (domestic pools), 82 leisure=swimming_area (natural bathing sites), and only 331 elements carrying a name.
  3. The count search surfaced a figure of 169 indoor and outdoor pools receiving swimming-lesson funding in 2023, and, more importantly, Landtag Drucksache 20/4417. The Bäderleben search established the database's scale (over 9,300 pools, 140+ attributes) and its search interface. The funding search established the §24 FAG mechanism at about 7.5 m EUR a year.

B.3 The Drucksache

  1. The Drucksache PDF was downloaded and extracted in layout mode: 7,480 lines. Its structure was read from the table of contents: infrastructure by type, condition and age, closures, renovation need, school swimming, plus two annexes.
  2. The Vorbemerkung was read in full and produced the pass's most consequential finding. The state holds no current inventory: the data lie with the municipalities, the state's figures rest on the 2016 survey and the 2019 Sportstättenstatistik, and the government states "die vorliegende Datenlage nicht auskömmlich ist bzw. aktualisiert werden muss". It then announces that the end-2026 FAG request will be run and expanded by the Statistikamt to collect counts per Kreis by type, basin counts and teaching basins, basin and water depths, locations, build year and last general renovation, current and decided renovations with costs, closures since 2006, and accessibility.
  3. The summary of the 2026 replies was read: 34 administrative units answered voluntarily, 32 are tallied, giving 82 facilities (25 Hallenbäder, 26 Freibäder, 12 Naturbäder, 19 Lehrschwimmbecken), 66 publicly accessible. This was recorded as a partial self-selected sample, explicitly not to be scaled.
  4. The free-text answers were scanned for heat-supply and energetic-renovation mentions and attributed to their responding municipality by tracking the section headers. Nine municipalities: Wesselburen (heat pump for pool water, 2023/24), Büdelsdorf (air-source heat pump plus photovoltaics), Süderbrarup (heat pump planned, 930,957 EUR), Plön (CHP-based supply renewed 2021-22), Bargteheide (external supplier plans to decarbonise the CHP), Heide (seeking industrial waste heat), Wyk auf Föhr (planning photovoltaics, heat pump, CHP and electricity), Kiel and Quarnstedt (photovoltaics). This was recorded as the entire public technical inventory of the state.

B.4 Anlage 2, and a parsing failure

  1. Anlage 2 was located and isolated: the 2025 FAG distribution, printing every funded facility with municipality, address, name, type, supervised-lesson flag, lesson hours, a Maßzahl and a funding amount, with printed totals of 126,342 hours and 7,499,999.94 EUR against a target of 7,500,000.
  2. A first parse on the layout-mode text used a regular expression for the numeric tail. It returned 167 rows but only 49.7% of the printed hours and 51.9% of the funding, with 94 rows unclassified by type.
  3. A second parse used fixed column offsets derived from the header. It reached 44.6% and 48.5%, worse, and produced an implausible type distribution.
  4. A third parse split on runs of whitespace and consumed the row from the right. It reached 44.6% of hours with 82 rows unclassified.
  5. The failure was diagnosed rather than patched further. Line lengths were measured: rows up to 85 were 256 characters, rows 87 onward were about 207. The landscape page is wider than the layout buffer, so the right-hand columns of the second half of the list were being clipped entirely, and on the boundary page the type column had slid by one row, mistyping for example "Freibad Quickborn" as Hallenbad.
  6. The document was re-extracted in reading order (pdftotext -raw). Each facility row appeared complete on one line. A single regular expression matched the whole row shape, and one page's worth of repeated rows was de-duplicated by running number.
  7. The fourth parse returned 166 rows and reconciled to 126,251 hours (99.93% of printed) and 7,494,597.94 EUR (99.93%), with the Maßzahl column summing to 0.9992, which independently confirms that the Maßzahl is each facility's share of the funding pool. One row of 167 remained unparsed, about 91 hours and 5,400 EUR. Logged as C7; the earlier half-total parses were discarded.

Electrification-conditional flexibility

Keeping a published method and changing one input: the fuel.

Part A · one-page method summary

Question
German reference demand-response accounting scores swimming baths at zero flexible electricity. Is that a property of the asset or an artefact of the method, and how large is the quantity the method cannot see?
Sources
Wohlfarth, Klobasa and Eßer, Energy Efficiency 12 (2019) 327-341, Tables 3 and 4; Schlomann et al. The counterfactual was therefore defined as: keep the method, replace fossil pool heat with heat pumps, convert heat to electricity at a seasonal performance factor, re-read the table. Comparisons would be theoretical to theoretical, which is the like-for-like comparison.

B.7 How the conclusion was derived

The chain of inference is short and each link is documented. The reference method's own definition of flexible appliances includes heat pumps (step 6). The second heat basis (step 14) guards against the acknowledged weakness of the first (step 13); both give the same qualitative answer.

B.8 Final conclusion

The reference method is blind to flexibility that exists only after a fuel switch. For German swimming baths the hidden quantity is 0.30 to 0.97 TWh/a of theoretical flexible electricity (central 0.83), comparable to the entire published potential of hospitals, with a flexible fraction above every listed subsector. Both zeros in the reference table (Baths, Laundries) are heat-and-water sectors, consistent with the blindness being to thermal flexibility under fossil supply rather than to baths in particular. The method under-counts every gas-heated building class in proportion to its heat demand; how much of the under-count is usable is asset-specific, and for pools it depends on the comfort and hygiene envelope (R4, and later R5's finding on the KOK design bands).

Status of the conclusion. VERIFIED as to the artefact and its order of magnitude. Estimate as to the exact figure (band stated). Not established: the technical revision, the electrification trajectory, stock heterogeneity, a measured performance factor, the interaction with existing CHP.

B.1 Origin, and why the premise was tested first

B.2 The premise fails

  1. §4 EnSikuMaV was read word for word. It prohibits heating "von privaten, nichtgewerblichen, innen- oder außenliegenden Schwimm- und Badebecken einschließlich Aufstellbecken mit Gas oder mit Strom aus dem Stromnetz". Private, non-commercial pools only.

R4

The evaporation loop

Calibrated against two anchors before any result was read.

Part A · one-page method summary

Question
Raising pool water temperature stores heat but increases evaporation, which both accelerates the leak and raises the electrical load of dehumidification. What does the loop cost, and does it decide whether pool-as-storage works?
Sources
VDI 2089 evaporation coefficients as reproduced in the Deutsche Gesellschaft für das Badewesen calculation note; Shah (2014), ASHRAE Transactions 120(2), SE-14-001, for the independent correlation; Benakcha, Labat, Hazyuk and Ginestet (2025), arXiv:2512.02586, the nearest prior work; Menerga ThermoCond 19/29 datasheet (2016) for dehumidification specific energy; Bekanntmachung Energieverbrauchswerte (2021) category 32 and UBA TEXTE 139/2024 as calibration anchors; AIVC and CIBSE for specific fan power.
Method
A first-order steady-state model of one 25 m pool (312.5 m² water, 562.5 m³, 654 kWh/K). Evaporation by the Dalton form used in VDI 2089, cross-checked against Shah. Additional losses from a charge: latent heat of the extra evaporation plus makeup-water heating. Vapour removal in two bracketing modes: winter fresh-air dilution (electricity is fan power, cost is thermal) and heat-pump condensing at datasheet specific energies (0.12 to 0.38 kWh/kg), with and without a pool-water condenser. Charging at seasonal performance 3.5 to convert stored heat into electricity shifted. The baseline was calibrated against two independent anchors before any result was read.
Result
Evaporation rises about 31% for a 2 K charge. For a +2 K charge (1,308 kWh stored, 374 kWh electricity shifted): overnight at rest, thermal loss 2 to 3% and electricity 0.5 to 4.4% of what was shifted; held through 12 bathing hours, thermal loss 19 to 25% and, in summer recirculation, electricity about 35% of what was shifted. A pool-water condenser cuts the thermal loss from 19% to 4% and does not change the electrical penalty. The penalty fraction is almost independent of charge depth.

B.10 How the conclusion was derived

The removal energy is taken from a real machine's datasheet at two operating modes (step 12), which brackets rather than assumes. The whole model was calibrated against an official benchmark and a national survey before any result was inspected (steps 18 to 19), so the numbers are anchored rather than free-floating. The conclusion then follows from reading one grid: the same charge costs a few percent overnight and a third of the shifted electricity when held through summer bathing hours (step 21). The seasonal alignment (step 22) is an observation about when the cheap regime coincides with the resource, not a modelled result.

B.11 Final conclusion

The evaporation loop does not decide whether pool-as-storage works; it decides the regime in which it works. Charging overnight at rest costs 2 to 3% of the stored heat and 0.5 to 4.4% of the electricity shifted. Holding a charge through bathing hours costs a fifth to a quarter of the stored heat, and in summer recirculation about a third of the electricity shifted, because the store leaks harder the harder it is charged and the leak runs the compressor. A pool-water condenser recovers the heat but not the electricity. The cost is set by mode, duration and season rather than by charge depth, which separates the envelope question from the loop question. Pool flexibility should therefore be dispatched as an overnight product, and an energy-system model treating pool storage as lossless overstates summer daytime flexibility by roughly a third in electrical terms. The nearest prior work states in its own text that this air-circuit consequence is unanalysed and deferred, so the gap is real by the nearest authors' own account.

Segment 2, storage. Segment 3, electrolysers and heat pumps of at least 100 kW, first commissioned after 29 December 2023, with no 2% rule. The reasoning notes that operation on a standard load profile is incompatible with the flexibility requirement.
  4. Concept sections 5 and 6 read. Installation requirements 5.2.1 a to f: commissioned; in a relief region; "einen eigenen Anschluss an das Netz der allgemeinen Versorgung"; "in ihrer Fahrweise flexibel (steuerbar und nicht lastprofilgebunden)"; at least 100 kW; no 6a contract. Loads under 100 kW may aggregate into a group of at least 100 kW within one region. The participant takes the role Einsatzverantwortlicher "unabhängig von den Eigentumsverhältnissen", signs the Rahmenvertrag with the TSO, builds and tests a communication channel (cyclic data, activation, confirmation), proves data provision for settlement.   5. Concept remuneration read. Monthly bid at or above a Mindestpreis fixed per calendar year; daily availability before 10:00; the participant procures its own energy; refund = max(reference price minus bid, 0) on the minimum of consumed and allocated energy, reference price = day-ahead capped at about 400 EUR/MWh; variable levies compensated; fixed grid charges compensated only if a TSO-set annual minimum availability is met, worked example 3,000 h; penalty = max(day-ahead minus bid, 0) on the unconsumed part of each hour's allocation. Mindestpreis in the 2027 example 21.68 EUR/MWh, derived as 43.37 × (1 minus 0.5) from a coal-CHP heat-cost comparison with a 50% discount.

B.3 Corrections to earlier claims arising from the texts

  1. C12. The scan, and summaries of it, had stated that installations under existing TSO contracts with CHP plants are excluded and that pools commonly run CHP, implying exposure. The statute and Tenor confine the exclusion to CHP plants holding a §13(6a) redispatch contract with a TSO; a pool's own CHP unit carries no such contract unless separately concluded.
  2. C13. The first gap map had written that the 2% full-load-hour rule "sits awkwardly against a near-constant load". The rule applies to the additional electric heater in Segment 1 (and to storage in Segment 2), not to the facility's total consumption, and not at all to a Segment 3 heat pump.
  3. C15. The 3,000-hour minimum availability had been described in passing as unavoidable. It is a precondition only for compensation of fixed grid charges; participation and the per-MWh refund do not depend on it.

B.7 How the conclusion was derived

The requirements are read directly from the three primary instruments (steps 3 to 6) and tabulated; nothing is inferred about what participation demands. The three veto points follow from three separately sourced facts: the steering-right transfer is a contract outside routine operation (step 11, forms and bodies); the hygiene duty sits on the operator by statute and is not transferable (step 13); a DSO confirmation is recorded in the concept (step 5). The temperature floor is shown to be guidance, not statute, by the absence of any statutory temperature and the presence of the KOK bands (steps 13 to 15), which then bound R4's envelope (step 16). The economics are a transparent product of price spread, volume and fixed cost (steps 17 to 19), sensitive mainly to headroom and allocated hours.

B.8 Final conclusion

The legal path for a municipal pool into §13k exists and is not exotic, but it runs through an aggregator taking the Einsatzverantwortlicher role and through three parties who can each stop it: the council or works committee that must resolve the contract and steering-right transfer, the operator whose IfSG §37 duty forces a reserved temperature floor into any such contract, and the connecting network operator. Water temperature is bounded by design guidance rather than statute, which makes it negotiable in a way hygiene is not and makes the storage envelope pool-type specific: sports pools at 28 °C have no upward headroom, children's and teaching pools have two to four kelvin. Single-site economics are marginal outside the best case of a 150 kW heat pump with headroom and full allocated hours; the willingness question is therefore a consolidation question, to be answered per municipality by legal form and decision body rather than by attitude survey.
