Swimming pools as flexible loads in Schleswig-Holstein

Six questions about public pools as power-to-heat flexibility, worked from public documents and open data. Each answer is reproducible from a script, each input carries a status, and each section says what the result does not establish.

Q0.1

What share of the curtailment can a distribution-connected pool reach?

Nearly all of it. The curtailment is caused in the transmission network, and that is the part §13k EnWG makes available to additional loads in a relief region. The part caused in the distribution network is about 3% and has no instrument.

Indicative. The 65% is a volume share from the state ministry report of 15 April 2026 (“über 65%”). The 90% applied to the remainder is a share of cases from an SH Netz paper of 2017 (“in über 90 Prozent der Fälle nicht im 110-kV-Netz”); SH Netz repeats the statement without the figure in 2022 and 2024. Both are lower bounds on transmission causation, so 28 GWh is an upper bound.

A second source agrees. §13k Abs. 8 EnWG lets large distribution operators run their own instrument for curtailment caused in their own network. The transmission operators' concept of 1 April 2026 records that the distribution operators see no case for it (“absehbar keinen eigenständigen Anwendungsfall”).

The future distribution case is in other places. SH Netz projects medium-voltage congestion in twelve county regions for 2045. The three counties with no §13k relief region (Ostholstein, Herzogtum Lauenburg, Plön) include the highest projected overload share.

  • The current split. The 90% figure is from 2017; a current number has to come from SH Netz.

  • If the projected congestion in each region is driven by load or by generation. The source table could not be read reliably for that column.

R1

Is the zero flexibility score for baths a property of the asset or of the method?

Of the method. The German reference assessment counts flexible electric appliances, and pool heat is fossil or district heat. With the heat electrified, the same method gives baths 0.30 to 0.97 TWh/a, central estimate 0.83 TWh/a.

The published table is Wohlfarth, Klobasa and Eßer, Energy Efficiency 12 (2019), 327-341, Table 4, after Schlomann et al. (2015). It gives baths 1.4 TWh/a of electricity and a flexible sum of 0.0. The authors' own list of flexible appliances includes heat pumps and electric water heating. The calculation changes one input, the fuel, and nothing else.

  • These are theoretical potentials, like Table 4 itself. The technical potential depends on the temperature band that hygiene and comfort allow.
  • The performance factors (3.0, 3.5, 4.5) are general ranges. No measured value for pool water was found.
  • Schlomann et al. state that their fuel figure for baths has a wider error band than ±15%.
  • If a later survey has replaced the 2013 data in this accounting.

R2

Does proximity to wind predict where curtailed energy can be claimed?

No. Proximity agrees with §13k eligibility for 24 of 76 indoor units (32%). Most eligible pools are far from turbines, and several pools in dense turbine clusters are excluded.

The transmission operators designate the §13k relief regions by county. In Schleswig-Holstein, region T5 is Dithmarschen and Nordfriesland. Region T6 is Neumünster, Lübeck, Pinneberg, Rendsburg-Eckernförde, Schleswig-Flensburg, Segeberg, Steinburg and Stormarn. Flensburg, Kiel, Plön, Ostholstein and Herzogtum Lauenburg are in no region. The operators give the reason: loads there would increase the congestion, or TenneT did not curtail there in the past.

Not established

  • Eligibility is a regulatory criterion at county level. It is not a map of distribution constraints.
  • Pools are located at town centroids, because the list prints the owner's address. A few units can move across the 5 km threshold.
  • Only one proximity measure was tested. A measure based on constrained substations needs network data that are not public.
  • Relief regions can change with six months' notice.

R4

What does the evaporation loop cost when heat is stored in pool water?

It sets the operating regime. It does not decide if storage is viable. An overnight charge loses 2 to 3% of the stored heat. A charge held through twelve bathing hours in summer costs about 35% of the shifted electricity in extra dehumidification.

Warmer water evaporates faster. The hall must then remove more moisture, and that uses electricity in the same building. The model is a steady-state balance for a 312.5 m² basin with a +2 K charge (1,308 kWh of heat, 374 kWh of electricity shifted at a performance factor of 3.5). Evaporation follows the VDI 2089 coefficients, cross-checked against the correlation of Shah (2014).

Thermal loss over the same windows: 2.3 to 3.1% overnight, 19 to 25% through bathing hours, and about 4% where a pool-water condenser returns the heat. Calibration before any result was read: the baseline gives 460 t/yr of evaporation and 55 to 175 MWh/yr of dehumidification electricity, within a factor of about 1.5 of two published anchors.

The nearest published study that tested pool overheating (Benakcha et al., 2025, arXiv:2512.02586) states that the effect on the air circuit is not analysed and is left to a later article.

Not established

  • Dynamics. The model is steady state: no heating rate, no stratification, no delay of the hall air.
  • Higher transmission and ventilation losses from a warmer hall. They make the penalty somewhat larger.
  • That +2 K is permitted. The design band for sports basins is 24 to 28 °C, so a pool at 28 °C has no upward margin. Teaching and children's basins (28 to 32 °C) have 2 to 4 K.
  • That no other model closes this loop. The search was not systematic, and Yuan et al. (2021) was not read.

R5

Who can commit a municipal pool to a grid signal, and does it pay?

Nobody alone. The council, the operator's hygiene duty and the connecting network operator each hold a veto. For a single site the value covers the fixed cost only in the best case.

PartyWhat it holdsBasis
Political body (council or works committee)Approval of a framework contract with the transmission operator and of the transfer of technical and legal control to an aggregatorGemeindeordnung SH and Eigenbetriebsverordnung. The exact list of reserved decisions was not read.
OperatorThe duty that pool water causes no harm to health. A contract cannot transfer it, so the operator must set the temperature limits.§37 (2) IfSG, read in full
Connecting network operatorA confirmation at registration, with a veto, was discussed with the distribution operatorsTransmission operators' concept v2.0. Recorded as discussed, not as adopted.

§13k requires a controllable electric heat source of at least 100 kW on its own metering point, alone or in a group inside one region. A heat pump first commissioned after 29 December 2023 qualifies without the 2% full-load-hour rule. A pool's own CHP unit is not excluded.

R3

The question that was not answered: winter 2022/23 as a natural experiment

The premise was wrong. Public pools were exempt from the federal energy-saving regulation, so the temperature reductions of that winter were not mandated.

§4 EnSikuMaV prohibited the heating of private, non-commercial pools with gas or grid electricity. Its explanatory note excludes pools that are open to the public for payment. The reductions that municipal pools made were decisions under cost pressure. The reports of recommendations by the bathing association and of what individual pools did come from press sources and are not verified.

A public series does exist: teaching hours per pool for 2023 (Landtag Drs. 20/2279, 169 facilities, reconciled exactly) and 2025. It starts after the episode, and the unit of observation changes between the years. It can show recovery from 2023 to 2025 after the units are reconciled. It cannot show a before-and-after effect. A pre-period needs operator records.
